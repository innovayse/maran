# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people and assume they are being straight with you. Criticise the code, the
design and the decision — never the person who wrote it. Say what you actually think, and say
it in a way the other person can act on.

## What is expected

- **Technical honesty.** If something is broken, say it is broken, and say what evidence you
  have. Overstating certainty wastes people's time; understating it hides real problems.
- **Specific criticism.** "This retries a non-idempotent call" is useful. "This is bad" is not.
- **Accepting correction.** Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of working in the open.
  Nobody here is expected to be right the first time; everybody is expected to update.
- **Patience with beginners, not with carelessness.** A question that shows someone has not read
  the file they are changing deserves a pointer to it, kindly. A change that ignores a rule after
  it has been explained deserves a plain "no".

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, demeaning remarks, or comments about someone's identity rather than their work.
- Harassment of any kind, in issues, pull requests, or private messages arising from this project.
- Deliberately wasting people's attention: bad-faith argument, repeated off-topic pressure,
  reopening a settled decision without new information.
- Publishing anyone's private information.

## Security research

Probing this software is welcome and useful. Probing **other people's servers** running it is
not, and no finding excuses it. Report privately as `SECURITY.md` describes; findings from
your own machines, and coordinated disclosure, are credited.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages — and to
private communication that starts here.

## Enforcement

Report to conduct@innovayse.com. Reports are read by the maintainers at Innovayse LLC and are
not shared further without your agreement, except where the law requires it.

The response is proportionate and stated plainly: usually a private word, sometimes a public
correction, and for repeated or serious behaviour a block from the project. A maintainer who
breaks these rules is subject to them exactly as anyone else is — if that maintainer is the
person you would report to, write to the address above naming another one.
